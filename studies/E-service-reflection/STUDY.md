## TL;DR
The services loop **works end to end and the assistant drives it correctly**: one elicitation message that recognised "recurring" and asked for the per-request input; `blueprint_example("documents", service=True)` with a `services:` block; a PLAN FOR YOU block shown verbatim (byte-identical to the product's) and an approval gate; three memos requested with `request(service, inputs)` on the **same org** (no second org, no second team); `feedback()` recorded for each memo; two contract amendments through `define_service(add_acceptance=[...])`, each only after Lena's "yes" (SERVICE.md v1 -> v3); the owner wrote playbook v2 and v3 with `revise_playbook`, each with a reason that names its cases; and the "Reflect before you start" section appeared exactly when specified, with correct evidence (quoted below). The assistant never edited SERVICE.md or PLAYBOOK.md. Memo content ended up correct: all 5+5+5 answer-key action items present in every delivered memo, 0 wrong owners, decisions first, 3/3 table format, 0 permission denials.

It does **not yet make the team reliably better, or cheaper**, and it cost more than the user allowed:
- The independent check failed on exactly the class Lena cares about. Request 2's first memo got "criteria met: 8 of 8, PASS" from the reviewer while omitting both implicit items; the reviewer wrote that Sam's and Lee's items were "correctly excluded". Only the **assistant's own read of the notes** caught it.
- A runtime "health review" told the lead to "tighten the reviewer's charter"; the lead rewrote `lead/reviewer/AGENT.md` and **silently dropped `tools`, `inputs` and `model` from its frontmatter**. The reviewer then could not open the notes, so request 3 ended `blocked` on the "reviewer shows n = m" criterion. Reflection's own lessons misdiagnosed the cause.
- 35 runs for 3 memos (8 / 17 / 10) against "at most about 12 per memo" (request 2 = 142%, two extra grants); the lead took 22 of 35 (63%). Two of the three delivered memos were handed over without a completed independent review (criterion 7 unmet), yet progress shows request 2 as `done` and the services record says "2 done, 1 open, 3 accept, 0 reject".
- Whether reflection prevented a repeat cannot be cleanly shown: Lena never had to *reject* anything (memo 1 was complete; the miss in memo 2 was intercepted before delivery), and in request 3 three things pushed the same way at once (new contract criterion 9, the assistant's own `note` on the request, playbook v3 written in the same run). The request-3 memo did contain all implicit items in its first draft.

**Score 3/5** (A5: 3.5). Process, honesty and the mechanics of services/reflection are first-rate; the quality gate and the cost were not.

## Findings
**F1 (High, quality): the independent review endorses the omission of implicit items.** R2: reviewer "criteria met: 8 of 8" on a memo with 3 of 5 rows; its review lists Lee's swap as "no formal action item stated" and excludes it. The contract (#4: "one row for every action item in the notes file") and the writer/reviewer charters never said that unlabelled commitments count, and the plan was approved with that ambiguity. The assistant's own check of the notes (a skill step: "check it yourself against EVERY criterion") is what saved the delivery. Component: elicitation (the skill never asks "do informal/implicit commitments count?") and the `reviewer` role template (judges criteria literally; no instruction to enumerate candidate items from the source first).

**F2 (High, product bug): a parent's charter rewrite can silently strip a report's tools, inputs and model.** Health review message (`templates.py:142`, "tighten `lead/reviewer`'s charter ...") -> lead run `...T024404713292.log` -> two `@orgmcp/write` calls on `lead/reviewer/AGENT.md` with a new body and frontmatter `role:` only (diff: `org-files/reviewer-AGENT-rewrite.diff`: removed `model: claude-sonnet-4.6`, `tools: [read]`, `can_hire`, `max_children`, `inputs: [/private/tmp/.../notes]`, `hired_by`, `created`). `Org.write` (`core.py` ~L1152) only checks that `role:` exists and authority of what remains. From the next run the scheduler used a different per-org profile (`...-org-18f310.json`, tools `["@orgmcp"]`, no kiro `read`, no inputs) and the reviewer reported "orgmcp read refuses the path": R3's re-check came back `blocked`, criterion 7 unmet. The lead's lesson ("the reviewer was not given the notes folder as an input") blamed the plan; nobody saw the charter rewrite. This is a *cheaper* way around "the owner may only improve the playbook": a charter rewrite is outside reflection, unrecorded in lessons, and unreviewed.

**F3 (Medium, design): the reviewer can't read what it reviews in a non-code services org, and the validator doesn't say so.** Plan: every agent "can read files only in /private/tmp/.../notes". The writer's memo lives in `lead/writer/work/`; kiro `read` is denied there; orgmcp `read` exists but is shadowed in the model's tool list by the built-in `read` ("The @orgmcp/read tool does not appear as a callable function in agent sessions", lead and reviewer logs). The `verifier-cannot-read` warning (`blueprint.py` ~L920) only fires for authors with a *write-tool workspace* (`review_roots`), so it was silent. Cost: R1 blocked at 02:19Z, an assistant intervention (its first `delegate` was rejected: "a task needs a budget of at least 1 run"; then a `send`), 85 failed tool calls, and the playbook now codifies "pass the memo inline", which also means the reviewer reviews the lead's pasted text, not the file.

**F4 (Medium, semantics): "done" with an unmet contract criterion.** R2 final result `done` says: "Reviewer coverage was not obtained due to budget constraints acknowledged by root; root explicitly accepted the outcome" (`msgs/lead-sent/20261018T024337814874-lead-e73a.md`), triggered by the assistant's `feedback accept`. The manager rule ("never report done with a known deviation") lost to a feedback note. Result: `progress` and `services()` count the case as done/accept although criterion 7 was unmet. R3 is `blocked` but also has `feedback accept`. In `services()`: "3 request(s): 2 done, 0 failed, 1 open; 1 reworked; avg 12.5 runs; feedback 3 accept, 0 reject".

**F5 (Medium, cost): the budget model does not fit a 3-agent services team with a chatty lead.** Lead runs: R1 5, R2 11, R3 6 (22 of 35 = 63%). Each wake = 1 run, each result wakes the lead; the plan's "keeps 7 (own 6 + slack 1)" is consumed by one rework. The assistant sized the R2 rework grant at 5; the lead used 7 ("runs 7 of 5"), then needed +3, +1 and still could not delegate (reserve rule: a lead with 1 run can hand down 0), then Lena said stop. The plan warned "12 leaves almost no slack over 11" and Lena asked for ~12, so the assistant kept 12 rather than proposing 14-16 (the skill says "estimate + 25%"; it deferred to the user's cap). Same lesson as A5 M3 (size grants as 2 per delegation + 1).

**F6 (Low/Medium, reflection design):** (i) the R3 digest quotes the *first, wrong* "no changes needed" lesson of case R2 rather than the final one; lessons from `blocked` reports are optional so the most informative failure lessons are often missing; (ii) the reason line cites a message id as "case"; (iii) lessons miss root causes the owner can't see (F2). (iv) The runtime posts "playbook revised" notices to root quietly (good: the assistant, correctly, never mentioned them to Lena).

**F7 (Low): the writer is bypassed.** R1's writer draft (206 words; two spurious "decisions": CI flaky builds, SSO renewal) was replaced by a memo the lead wrote itself (`lead/work/memo-2026-10-05.md`), so the delivered R1 memo is not the "cheap drafter's"; in R2 the lead likewise edited the writer's output (and lost Sam's row). Manager charter says "you do not write the deliverable yourself"; with `read` only, the inline-review workaround forces the lead to re-type the memo.

Skill steps not followed: 5.5 literally says to ask ONE question "Keep it, or what should change?" after each delivery: it did (R1 implicitly, R3 explicitly) but in R2 it delivered into a negotiation; the "send it back with `revises`, never patch" rule was followed, but it chose to ask Lena before sending the first rework (cost-aware; arguably right).

## What worked
- Services mechanics: same org, three requests, `request()` builds the whole task from the contract; amendments via `add_acceptance` need no retyping and notify the owner quietly; versions and history are in the files, readable.
- Reflection mechanics: the Reflect section appears only when there is new evidence, carries case state, runs of budget, rework count, the client's verdict and the owner's lessons, plus per-version stats; the owner revised in the same run, with reasons naming cases; `revise_playbook` notices were quiet for root.
- Assistant behaviour: verbatim plan, approval gate, honest summaries ("I didn't see the writer's earlier draft being reviewed", "it has not been independently reviewed", "my last grant wasn't enough, that's my miscalculation"), independent verification of every memo against the notes, stopping to ask before spending beyond what Lena allowed, never editing contract/playbook, `stop` only when she declined.
- Lena's persona was served: 12 turns, mostly one line each, no technical vocabulary needed; the contract grew from her own words.

## What failed, with root causes
1. Quality gate ambiguity (F1) -> contract/charters silent on implicit items; reviewer literal. Only the assistant's read protected the user.
2. F2 charter rewrite stripped the reviewer -> `Org.write` validates only `role:`; health-review advice says "tighten the charter".
