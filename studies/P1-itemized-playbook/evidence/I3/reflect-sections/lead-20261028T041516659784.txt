The client rejected a delivery: check it against their words, the regression list and the contract before you plan the rework. Without the client's evidence (step 3) a rejection is a reason to look, not to change an item: your parent asks the client whether it is a lasting requirement, and the answer reaches you as a contract change or a criterion link.
1. Look for what repeats, and for anything the client rejected (the regression list). A rejection or a failure is enough to look into on its own; a single slow case is not.
2. When a lesson of yours conflicts with the client's own feedback (not a relayed verdict), the client is right: apply the feedback in this rework.
3. If a change to how the team works would prevent it, change one item with `revise_playbook` (its description gives the ops). A rule, or a step that changes a check, an output or how a criterion is handled, needs evidence in `evidence`: the same criterion or assertion failed in 2 different cases the client rejected, or the item is the own words of a criterion the client confirmed (now C10, C12 (an item may be the own words of one)). Lessons and relayed verdicts are not evidence. An admitted item is watched: a related client rejection warns it, a second retires it. Retiring or weakening an item that covers a criterion needs your parent's acknowledgement. If a change is refused, nothing is written: send your parent its question once and go on.
4. The playbook may raise the bar, never lower it: the acceptance criteria come from the contract and travel with every request. Your parent asks the client whether a rejection is a lasting requirement: send nothing about it yourself beyond a refused change's question (step 3).
5. If nothing needs to change, change nothing. Then handle the rework following the playbook.
