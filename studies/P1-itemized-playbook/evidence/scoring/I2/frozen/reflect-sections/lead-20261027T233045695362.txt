Before working on the new request:
1. Look for what repeats, and for anything the client rejected (the regression list). A rejection or a failure is enough to look into on its own; a single slow case is not.
2. When a lesson of yours conflicts with the client's own feedback (not a relayed verdict), the client is right: apply the feedback in this request.
3. If a change to how the team works would prevent it, change one item with `revise_playbook` (its description gives the ops). A rule, or a step that changes a check, an output or how a criterion is handled, needs evidence in `evidence`: the same criterion or assertion failed in 2 different cases the client rejected, or the item is the own words of a criterion the client confirmed (now C8 (an item may be the own words of it)). Lessons and relayed verdicts are not evidence. An admitted item is watched: a related client rejection warns it, a second retires it. Retiring or weakening an item that covers a criterion needs your parent's acknowledgement. If a change is refused, nothing is written: send your parent its question once and go on.
4. The playbook may raise the bar, never lower it: the acceptance criteria come from the contract and travel with every request. Your parent asks the client whether a rejection is a lasting requirement: send nothing about it yourself beyond a refused change's question (step 3).
5. If nothing needs to change, change nothing. Then handle the request following the playbook.
