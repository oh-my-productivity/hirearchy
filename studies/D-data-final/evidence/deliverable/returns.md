# What is driving product returns in 2025?

## What the return rate means

The return rate is the number of returned orders divided by all orders in the group. Each row of the orders file is one order, and "returned" is simply yes or no. For example, the shop had 3000 orders and 216 were returned, so the overall return rate is 7.2%. A rate is only as solid as the number of orders behind it, so every table shows both.

The data was clean: no blank values in any field except the return reason, which is only filled in for returned orders (and every returned order has one), no duplicate orders, and every order dated in 2025.

## Three key findings

**1. One combination stands out: Home Textiles sold on the marketplace.** Those orders were returned 37 times out of 122 orders, a rate of 30.3%. Every other order together was returned at 6.2% (179 out of 2878). That small slice is only 122 of the 3000 orders but accounts for 37 of the 216 returns. Within it, Linen throw (15 of 33, 45.5%) and Wool blanket (10 of 26, 38.5%) are the worst.

**2. The "problem category" and "problem channel" owe a lot to this one combination.** Home Textiles has the highest category rate at 13.1%, and the marketplace has the highest channel rate at 9.1%. But Home Textiles sold on the web or app is returned at only 6.7% (22 of 329), and marketplace orders for all other categories are returned at 5.8% (45 of 779). Take out the one combination and these two headline rates shrink a lot (to 6.7% and 5.8%). Furniture is different: it stays high at 10.0% (24 of 239).

**3. Expensive items are returned more often, most clearly on the web.** Orders with a unit price of 100 and over were returned 55 times out of 568 orders (9.7%), against 27 out of 556 orders (4.9%) for items under 20. The pattern holds with the Home Textiles marketplace orders taken out: 52 of 540 (9.6%) against 26 of 554 (4.7%). On the web the gap is clear: 32 of 256 (12.5%) against 10 of 261 (3.8%). On the marketplace the gap is modest: 16 of 169 (9.5%) against 11 of 155 (7.1%). In the app it is barely there: 7 of 143 (4.9%) against 6 of 140 (4.3%). Region and quantity show no clear story: regions range only from 6.8% to 7.6%, and the quantity groups are uneven and small at the top (10.0% for 4 units, but only 100 orders).

## Why customers say they return things

The stated reasons are spread fairly evenly across the 216 returns: Not as described 52 (24.1%), Late delivery 46 (21.3%), Wrong size/colour 45 (20.8%), Changed mind 42 (19.4%), Arrived damaged 31 (14.4%). For the Home Textiles marketplace returns, "Not as described" is the most common reason (13 of 37, 35.1%), followed by "Wrong size/colour" (9 of 37, 24.3%). Both could point at how the product is presented and sized.

## Three recommendations

1. **Check the Home Textiles marketplace listings first, starting with Linen throw and Wool blanket.** Compare the photos, descriptions, sizes and colour names with what customers actually receive, and correct whatever is misleading.
2. **Look into Home Textiles marketplace orders in the South and West.** Within Home Textiles marketplace orders, the return rates are 39.5% (17 of 43) in the South and 37.0% (10 of 27) in the West, against 24.1% (7 of 29) in the North and 13.0% (3 of 23) in the East. Check whether a particular carrier, warehouse or listing is behind it.
3. **Add clearer size and product details to high-priced items and Furniture, and keep tracking them.** Use items priced 100 and over (9.7%) and Furniture (10.0%) as the watch list, and re-run this analysis next quarter to see whether the changes worked.

## Where the data shows a pattern but cannot prove a cause

- The Home Textiles marketplace spike is clear, but the file only says it happened, not why. A product description problem, a different kind of customer or a handling issue would all look the same here.
- The regional gap inside that spike rests on small groups (23 to 43 orders each), so treat it as a lead to investigate, not a conclusion.
- Higher return rates for expensive items might come from the price itself, from the types of product that cost more, or from higher customer expectations. The data cannot separate these.
- The stated reasons are what customers chose from a list. They may not be the real reason.
- This covers 2025 orders only, with no cost, margin or customer information, so it cannot say how much the returns cost.
