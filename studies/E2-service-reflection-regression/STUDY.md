## TL;DR
**Score 4/5 (E: 3/5).** All six fixes hold in a real run, and the team's own work was clean: in 35 runs no review passed a memo that missed an answer-key item, no `done` was reported with a known unmet criterion, the reviewer read the writer's file directly (1 failed tool call in 35 runs vs 85 in E), no charter lost its frontmatter, and the two memos after the rejection kept the "Summary:" line, the section order and the informal items **without Lena or the assistant restating them to the writer**. The delivered memos end up fully right (8/8 decisions, 15/15 action items, 0 wrong dates, 0 extra rows, Summary first 3/3).

What keeps it from a 5 is no longer the team, it is the loop around the team:
- **The reflection digest erases the rejection.** Lena rejected twice (memo 1, memo 3). The lead's "Reflect before you start" sections before request 2 and 3 say "feedback 1 accept, 0 reject" and show `accept "User: this version is right."`; her rejection words appear nowhere in them. `services()` ends with "feedback 3 accept, 0 reject". Cause: `cases()` keeps only the last feedback of a rework chain (`services.py` ~L862 `fb[-1]`), and the reject sat on a task that the assistant had sent back with `request(revises=...)`.
- **The assistant overruled a correct, reviewed memo and then had to be rejected for it.** Memo 1's reviewed version (reviewer PASS 8/8) was exactly the answer key. The assistant, applying its own criterion "a date is never invented" literally, sent it back to turn Sam's "before Friday" and Marta's "for Oct 14" into `none` (5 runs, unasked), told Lena "the team's reviewer missed it", and delivered the worse memo. Lena's first rejection was therefore the assistant's doing, not the team's. The memo then cost **19 runs against the 15 Lena had approved**; the assistant reported "5 of 6" for the last rework and never the total.
- **Reflection can lock in a wrong rule.** Playbook v2 (written by the lead before Lena's rejection, from the assistant's wrong rework) says informal dates must be `none`; the lead's lesson after the rejection even says "'before Friday' means Thursday". It was undone in v3 only because the contract amendment arrived and the assistant's request note told the lead to fix it. After the second rejection (memo 3, "Marta keeps the agenda as sent") the lead's lessons say "Playbook v3 is solid" and the playbook was not revised; the fix lives in contract criterion 10 only.

## Findings
**F-E2-1 (High, product): the rejection never reaches the digest or the record.** `services.py` `cases()` builds `feedback=fb[-1]` (the last feedback note on the chain, ~L862) and the `feedback()` docstring says "a later feedback on the same case replaces this one in the statistics". Memo 1: `reject` filed on rework 1 (`d90e`), then `accept` on rework 2 (`ccf0`): the case shows `accept`. Same for memo 3. Effects: both Reflect sections (`feedback 1 accept, 0 reject`), the playbook-version statistics, and `services()` ("3 accept, 0 reject") understate quality problems; "Look for ... anything the client rejected" (the digest's own step 1) has nothing to find. The very fact the F6 fix wanted ("shows the rejection with Lena's words") is lost whenever the user rejects a rework instead of the original. Even a rejected-then-fixed case is information: it is the signal that the first delivery was not right.

**F-E2-2 (High, skill/assistant): the assistant overrode a correct, independently PASSed memo with its own reading of an ambiguous criterion, and misattributed the miss.** Sequence: reviewer v1 FAIL (Sam 2026-10-08), rework, reviewer v2 **PASS 8/8** on exactly the answer-key memo (`memos/versions/2026-10-05.v2.bca89db.md`); the lead reported `done` ("Sam's Due is 2026-10-09 - met"). The assistant read the memo and notes and decided that "2026-10-09" for "before Friday" and "2026-10-14" for "for Oct 14" violate its own criterion 6 ("a date is never invented"), sent a rework request telling the lead to use `none` (`msgs/root-sent/20261018T035429740646-root-d90e.md`), got a worse memo, delivered it, and said "the team's reviewer missed it". Lena rejected it. Root causes: (1) elicitation never asked how relative dates should be handled although the skill now asks about informal items (SKILL.md step 1, item 3); (2) the assistant authored criterion 6 and the "Open questions" concept (a default Lena accepted without reading it); (3) skill step 5.1-5.2 ("check it yourself against EVERY criterion ... if unmet send it back") has no rule for an ambiguous criterion where the independent reviewer evidenced a PASS: the right move was one question to Lena, not an unrequested 5-run rework; (4) the E-era lesson "the assistant's own check catches what the reviewer misses" is true for omissions but not for judgement calls.

**F-E2-3 (Medium, product/design): reflection can write a wrong rule into the playbook, and the digest then shows the wrong lesson as the latest.** v2 pitfall came from the lead's lesson on the assistant's rework; the digest before request 2 shows the lead's lesson ("'before Friday' means Thursday ...") next to the client's accept, with no marker that the client disagreed. The prompt's own rule 4 ("If the current version did worse than an earlier one, restore that one") cannot fire because there is no per-version quality signal other than accept/reject counts (F-E2-1). The lead also revised its playbook outside the Reflect flow (end of a run, from its own lesson) without any check against feedback. `revise_playbook`'s only guard is "never lower the bar", not "does the client's text agree".

**F-E2-4 (Medium, cost disclosure): a rework chain is not capped, and the total is not told.** `request(revises=..., budget=6)` is a fresh budget per rework; memo 1 was granted 15 + 6 + 6 = 27 and used 19. The assistant told Lena after the first rework "14 in total", after the second only "this rework used 5 of 6" (turn 5), never "19 against the 15 you approved". The skill says spend beyond the approved budget needs OK (step 4) and that step 5 reply shows "runs used of planned"; no rule covers rework chains or says to state the case total. Lena's reject made the second rework implicitly welcome, but the first (assistant-initiated, 5 runs for nothing) was never put to her. `progress` shows per-task runs only ("runs 5 of 6"), not per case.

**F-E2-5 (Medium, product): the F1 fix moves the failure from omission to over-inclusion, and the reviewer's `ambiguous` bucket is not escalated.** "Marta will keep the agenda as sent" was listed as a commitment by the lead's own enumeration, by the reviewer ("Marta - keep the agenda as sent (no date -> none)", PASS 8/8) and by the writer, then rejected by Lena. The reviewer template says to list unclear items as `ambiguous` and report them; here the reviewer decided silently ("exactly 6 rows, one per commitment"). Contract criterion 5 pulls both ways ("every commitment ... including informal ones" vs "no row for something nobody committed to"). Cost of the fix: one more rework (5 runs) and a criterion.

**F-E2-6 (Low, cosmetic/product):** (a) the product instruction "(show this to the user exactly as written)" and "END OF PLAN FOR YOU" were echoed to Lena twice; (b) `&lt;weekday&gt;` HTML escaping in a stored playbook reason (shown by `services()`); (c) health `low_quality` counts user-driven reworks of a case against the reviewer (`health.py` ~L183: "(failed + blocked + rework) / tasks received", 3 of 5), which triggered a charter edit that was not needed (frontmatter safe, but the lead spent a run); (d) playbook v1 text "On this first run delegate only the steps that need nothing from a teammate" survives into v3, a template leftover; (e) the reviewer's "criteria met n of m" counts 8 of 8 against a 9-10 criterion contract (it excludes its own report criterion), so m never equals the contract's count.

**What now works (verified):** F1 enumeration, F2 frontmatter, F3 direct reads (`allowedPaths` = notes + org; org_read used 13 times, no shadowing), F4 wording of the feedback header and the skill rule, F5 sizing (rework 6 used 5; 15 per memo suffices, 0 stalls), F6 lessons present and latest-first. The 24-minute, 9-turn run is much smoother than E.
