The client rejected a delivery: check the delivery against its words, the regression list and the contract before you plan the rework. One rejection is a reason to look, not to change how the team works: your parent asks the client whether it is a lasting requirement, and the answer reaches you as a contract change or a criterion link. Revise the playbook only as step 3 says, then handle the rework.
1. Look for what repeats, and for anything the client rejected (the regression list). A rejection or a failure is enough to look into on its own; a single slow case is not.
2. When a lesson of yours conflicts with the client's own feedback (not a relayed verdict), the client is right: apply the feedback in this rework, and change the playbook only as step 3 says (say so in the reason).
3. If a change to how the team works would prevent it, call `revise_playbook(service, content, reason, evidence)` with the whole new playbook (at most 1000 words): concrete steps (who does what, in which order, which check), not slogans. Name the cases in the reason, and list in `evidence` what it rests on: regression assertions (R<k>), criteria (C<k>), verdict or case ids. It is admitted only when the same criterion or assertion failed in 2 different cases the client rejected, or when it cites a criterion your parent added to the contract by an amendment; your lessons and relayed verdicts (the assistant's own reading) are not evidence. If it is refused, nothing is written: send your parent the refusal's question once and go on with the playbook as it is. The new version applies to this rework.
4. The playbook may raise the bar, never lower it: the acceptance criteria come from the contract and travel with every request. Your parent asks the client whether a rejection is a lasting requirement: send nothing about it yourself beyond a refused revision's question (step 3).
5. If nothing needs to change, do not revise. Then handle the rework following the playbook.
