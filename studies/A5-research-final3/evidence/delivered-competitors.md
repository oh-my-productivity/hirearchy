## Where we can win

*Our assessment (judgement)*

The self-hosted note-taking market reveals clear strategic opportunities:

**Open-source as competitive advantage:** A substantial portion of this market operates on open-source licensing, meaning users retain full transparency and control over their data. Proprietary alternatives succeed through superior user experience rather than licensing restrictions. This suggests that open-source positioning alone does not guarantee market share—execution and design matter more.

**Sync fragmentation creates opportunity:** No single sync model dominates the market. Competitors offer a range of approaches: proprietary cloud services, third-party file integration, self-hosted servers, and combinations thereof. This fragmentation means sync is not a primary purchasing decision; users accept diverse solutions, suggesting room for a differentiated approach that aligns with a specific deployment philosophy.

**Team collaboration underserved:** While most platforms focus on individual note-taking, few address collaborative workflows directly. The platforms offering team-focused pricing represent niche segments, indicating unmet demand for platforms that prioritize collaboration from the ground up rather than as an add-on.

**Pricing accessibility drives adoption:** The most successful competitors offer free core products with optional premium features, creating low barriers to entry. Users choose upgrades when their needs expand. This model attracts individual users who become advocates and can convert to paying customers if collaboration or hosting needs emerge.

**Self-hosting demand is growing:** Several competitors have responded to user demand for self-hosted deployments, indicating this segment is gaining importance. Organizations prioritize data sovereignty and want alternatives to cloud-only services. A platform that simplifies self-hosting and reduces operational overhead would address this demand.

**Licensing flexibility matters:** Open-source platforms with permissive licensing (like MIT) face fewer adoption barriers in enterprise contexts than restrictive licenses. Yet proprietary platforms with strong features succeed too, suggesting licensing is one factor among many, not the deciding criterion.

In summary, the most differentiated opportunity lies in combining straightforward self-hosting, transparent and simple pricing, and native team collaboration features. The market has proven demand for local-first approaches, but no leader has fully integrated all three elements in a way that feels effortless to both individual and team users.
